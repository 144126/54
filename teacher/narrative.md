# the priest who came back

two pictures. same faith. very different rooms.

picture one. jerusalem, about the year 40. a house full of people. bread on the table.
somebody reads a letter. somebody prays. somebody explains a hard part of it. when a
family sells land, the money goes to the group, and it is shared out "as any one had
need" (acts 2:45). no stage. no paid man at the front. just a family with work to do.

picture two. today. a building. a pulpit at the front. one man stands there every
week. he teaches, he preaches, he visits, he blesses, he leads. the others sit and
watch. he is paid a salary, and the salary is owed to him. his title is preacher or
minister.

same book behind both pictures. only one of them is in the book.

## what the first church actually had

it had shared meals and shared money. "had all things common" (acts 2:44).

it had elders. that just means church leaders chosen from among the members, always
several, never one. "having appointed to them by vote elders in every assembly" (acts
14:23). the men who had been there longest, who could teach, did the teaching.

it had teaching spread all over the place. old women taught young women (titus 2:3-4).
parents taught children (ephesians 6:4). anyone with a gift for teaching, taught. the
writer of hebrews told the whole church: "because of the time ye ought to be teachers"
(hebrews 5:12). grown people were expected to teach, not to be kept on milk forever.

and it had visitors. when a young church had no leaders yet, someone was sent to sort
it out. titus was left in crete "that the things lacking thou mayest arrange, and
mayest set down in every city elders" (titus 1:5). he set the church up, and then he
left. his job was to make himself unneeded. that is a repairman, not a staff member.

## what the first church did not have

a hired man. nobody in the book collects a paycheck from one church for standing in
front of it.

it is not that workers got nothing. workers were supported, freely. "worthy is the
workman of his hire" (luke 10:7). those taught were told to share with those who teach
(galatians 6:6). paul took money from other churches while serving this one for free
(2 corinthians 11:8). but giving had to be free: "not out of sorrow or out of
necessity" (2 corinthians 9:7). a fixed, owed, compulsory salary is exactly "out of
necessity". and the leaders were warned not to serve "for filthy lucre" (1 peter 5:2).
for money is the one motive leaders must not have.

## the job god cancelled

way back, one family did all the holy work in israel. the levites. they had no land
and no job outside it, so god gave them the tithes, a tenth of everything (numbers
18:21). one tribe, one class of priests, paid by the people to stand between them and
god. that was the deal.

then jesus came, and the deal changed. "the priesthood being changed, of necessity
also, of the law a change doth come" (hebrews 7:12). the old command was cancelled
(hebrews 7:18). the tithes-to-pay-the-priest rule went with it.

in its place: everybody is a priest now. "a holy priesthood" (1 peter 2:5). "a royal
priesthood" (1 peter 2:9). no tribe, no class, no one standing between you and god.
that is not a small change. that is the whole system, taken apart.

## how he crept back

now look at picture two again.

one man, set apart from the others. paid by tithes. a raised platform where only he
stands. a title only he may carry, though jesus said "all ye are brethren" (matthew
23:8). the rest of the family sits and watches him do the work god spread to everyone.

that is the levitical priest. cancelled in hebrews 7, back at the front of the room,
wearing a suit.

the one thing the new deal was built to remove is the one thing every church now pays
for. that is transference: taking the rules of the old deal and moving them into the
new one where they no longer belong.

## the test

ask one question about any church arrangement: is this sharing, or is this payroll?

sharing: free, given "as any one had need", sent when someone is working, sometimes
refused by the worker himself. paul did that, with his own hands (acts 20:34).

payroll: owed, fixed, compulsory, forever, one man doing the work the family was
given to do.

one is in the book. the other one hired the priest back.

so the question for any church is not "can we afford the preacher". it is "who told us
to put him back at the front, alone?".
