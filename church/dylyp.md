DO YOU LOVE YOUR PREACHER?

Galatians 6:6; 1 Corinthians 9:14-16

Today, I want to ask a very uncomfortable question: Do you love your preacher?

And I am not asking whether you greet him, shake his hand, sit beside him in church or call him “Brother.” I am asking whether your love for the man who feeds you spiritually has ever translated into anything tangible.

Galatians 6:6 says, “Let him that is taught in the word communicate unto him that teacheth in all good things.” Look at the context carefully. The person teaching the church is principally the preacher, the one charged with feeding the congregation with the Word, rebuking, exhorting, correcting and helping the church to grow. He may involve other teachers, but that does not remove his principal responsibility as the preacher and teacher of the congregation.

Paul makes the same principle clear in 1 Corinthians 9:14: “Even so hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live of the gospel.”

So why have some of us developed a mindset that the preacher should be treated as though he is simply another person who happens to have a microphone on Sunday?

Yes, the preacher is your brother. Yes, he is equal with you before God. But that does not mean that everybody has the same responsibility, office, function or honour among men.

Your schoolmate may become a governor. Your classmate may become a permanent secretary. You may know him personally, but you cannot walk into his office anyhow because his position carries responsibility and protocol.

The same principle applies in the church.

The preacher is a servant of God entrusted with a spiritual responsibility. Do not reduce his office to “he is just one of us.”

WHAT HAVE YOU GIVEN YOUR PREACHER?

Let me ask you directly.

You have been a Christian for five, ten, fifteen or twenty years.

What have you personally done for your preacher?

Not what the congregation gives him.

You.

Have you bought him clothes?

Have you bought him shoes?

Have you taken food to his house?

Have you helped with his children's school fees?

Have you given him money simply because you appreciate his labour?

Have you ever looked at your preacher and said, “Brother, I just want to bless you”?

Somebody will say, “But I don't have much.”

The Shunammite woman did not wait until she became wealthy before she became a blessing to the man of God.

It is not always about the size of what you have.

It is about your heart and your willingness.

“I DON'T LIKE MY PREACHER”

Then somebody says, “I don't give my preacher because I am not happy with him.”

Fine.

Let me ask you one question:

Have you given another preacher?

If your answer is no, then perhaps your problem is not the preacher.

Perhaps your problem is your heart.

Somebody once said about his preacher, “I will not fetch water and pour it into the river.”

That is an interesting statement.

But if you believe your preacher already has enough, have you taken your water to another preacher who has none?

Have you?

If not, then stop using the preacher's supposed prosperity as an excuse for your own unwillingness to give.

SOME OF US HAVE LOST OUR PRIORITIES

Some people will spend heavily on their cars, phones, clothes, parties, dogs, domestic workers and personal comfort, but when it is time to care for the man who feeds their souls, suddenly everybody becomes an economist.

When the congregation proposes an increase in the preacher's support, arguments begin.

“Why?”

“Can't he manage what he has?”

“Things are hard for everybody.”

Yes, things are hard for everybody.

That is exactly why we should be looking for ways to help one another.

The preacher is also paying school fees.

The preacher is also buying food.

The preacher is also paying transport.

The preacher also has children.

The preacher also gets sick.

The preacher also has needs.

He is not a spiritual robot.

I once heard of a situation where a member's gateman and driver allegedly earned more than the preacher.

Whether that particular story is true is another matter.

But ask yourself:

Can that ever happen among God's people?

If it can, then something has gone seriously wrong with our priorities.

LOVE MUST BECOME TANGIBLE

If you love your preacher, let your love become tangible.

Buy him a shirt.

Buy him a belt.

Buy him a tie.

Get his measurement and give it to a tailor.

Buy him shoes.

Take food to his house.

Help with a child's school fees.

Give him books.

Help with transportation.

Send something to him unexpectedly.

You do not need to be rich to do these things.

One hundred members deciding to bless their preacher in different ways can completely change his experience.

And remember, the Bible does not demand that everybody give the same thing.

Give according to your ability.

The question is not, “How much does Brother Mike need?”

The first question is:

“What can I do?”

APPRECIATION IS NOT ONLY MONEY

But let me make something very clear.

Preacher support is not only about money.

Some of you genuinely do not have much money. I understand that.

But some of you don't have money and you don't have appreciation.

You cannot say:

“God bless you, preacher.”

“You are doing well.”

“Thank you for the message.”

“The Lord is your strength.”

“I appreciate your labour.”

These things cost nothing.

Words of affirmation can strengthen a tired preacher more than you realise.

Sometimes a preacher finishes preaching and nobody says anything.

He writes an article online and his own brethren read it silently.

You read it.

You benefit from it.

You close your phone.

No “Like.”

No comment.

No sharing.

No “Brother, this blessed me.”

Nothing.

But let somebody post a joke, a political argument or a sensational story, and suddenly everybody has strength to comment and share.

Why?

If your preacher is doing something good, let him know you see it.

Appreciation is encouragement.

Encouragement is support.

And sometimes the greatest gift you can give a preacher is to let him know that his labour is not invisible.

STOP CRITICISING WHAT YOU REFUSE TO SUPPORT

Some people have plenty to say about the preacher.

His sermons are not good enough.

His dressing is not good enough.

His leadership is not good enough.

His family is not good enough.

His personality is not good enough.

But what have you done to help him become better?

You want a polished preacher, but you refuse to contribute to the polishing.

You want a preacher who looks presentable, reads good books, travels to seminars, has good technology, dresses well and performs excellently, but you don't want to invest in him.

You cannot continually withdraw from a man and complain that he is running empty.

If you polish your preacher, you will be proud to introduce him.

If you support him, you strengthen his hands.

If you encourage him, you make his burden lighter.

If you care for him, you demonstrate that you value the work he is doing.

And please understand this: supporting your preacher does not mean worshipping your preacher.

The preacher is not above correction.

The preacher is not perfect.

The preacher can make mistakes.

Correct him when necessary.

But don't use his imperfections as an excuse to become deliberately ungenerous toward him.

PREACHER SUPPORT CONSCIOUSNESS

We need to develop what I call preacher support consciousness.

Some people have it naturally.

I once heard of people travelling abroad who, immediately they received their allowance, one person said, “I am going to buy suit for my daddy.” The person is a man from a denomination that we roundly criticise. 

That statement revealed something.

The person had preacher-support consciousness.

Some Christians don't.

We have developed preacher-criticism consciousness.

Preacher-expectation consciousness.

Preacher-demand consciousness.

But not preacher-support consciousness.

That must change.

The preacher is giving you spiritual things.

The Bible says those who are taught should communicate good things to the one teaching them.

So don't wait for the congregation alone.

You personally can become a blessing to your preacher.

And if every member begins to think this way, the preacher will not lack.

THE PREACHER SUPPORT BENCHMARK

Now, before you leave this message, use the Preacher Support Benchmark and evaluate yourself.

Ask yourself: As of today, what have I done to bless my preacher?

Not only money.

Have I encouraged him?

Have I appreciated him?

Have I prayed for him?

Have I shared his good work?

Have I helped him materially?

Have I strengthened his hands?

Then put yourself on a scale of 1 to 10.

1 means you have practically done nothing.

10 means you have developed a consistent personal habit of supporting, encouraging and blessing your preacher according to your ability.

Give yourself a mark.

Don't give yourself the mark you wish you had.

Give yourself the mark you honestly deserve.

Where have you reached?

And if your answer is 2, 3, 4 or 5, don't become defensive.

Become better.

Start now.

Pick up your phone and call him.

You know what to tell him.

Dr. Michael Udam, the village preacher, serves the church of Christ meeting at Ukpagada Ogoja Nigeria
